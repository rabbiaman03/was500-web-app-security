Six Figures Who Shaped Security

Alan Turing

1912–1954

Breaking the Enigma cipher at Bletchley Park.

His work proved that machine-scale attacks could break ciphers,a lesson that still sets the bar for how strong modern encryption has to be.

Cryptanalysis

Claude Shannon

1916–2001

Founding information theory.

His mathematical definition of perfect secrecy is the piller of every cipher. From one-time pads to modern block ciphers it is still measured against.

Information Theory

Grace Hopper

1906–1992

Compilers, and popularising the term "debugging".

By making it possible to write software in readable language instead of raw machine code, she made securable software possible at all.

Programming Languages

Elizebeth Smith Friedman

1892–1980

Breaking smuggling and spy ciphers for the US Coast Guard.

She showed that cryptanalysis could stand up as courtroom evidence and trained the generation of codebreakers who carried her methods into later intelligence work.

Cryptanalysis

Whitfield Diffie

b. 1944

Co-inventing public-key exchange.

His idea let two strangers agree on a shared secret over a public channel, which is the reason every HTTPS connection today works without a pre-shared key.

Public-Key Cryptography

Martin Hellman

b. 1945

Co-inventing public-key exchange.

Beyond the algorithm itself, he spent decades publicly arguing for stronger key lengths and against weakening encryption.

Public-Key Cryptography
